# About Me

I was born and raised in a bilingual bicultural family; and though I have always lived in France, I grew up regularly visiting family and friends in the USA, and have spent most of my professional life working in English with bilingual teams.

## Personal Philosophy

I thrive on creation. Throughout my life, I have always been in love with the process of turning curiosity and the need for understanding into a passion for building, designing, and tinkering.  
This is what has guided me through my various endeavors, and what is the primary driving force behind all my projects, both work-related and personal.

I have always considered myself to be very curious, and happy to learn and explore new ideas with others.

### Open Source

I am an open source and free software convert; and strongly believe in the importance of a free and open internet. Numerous projects I've created over the years are available on [my GitHub page](https://github.com/ELowry), and I have occasionally contributed to other, larger, projects when possible.

I hope to be able to continue contributing to the broader ecosystem for the foreseeable future.

### I'm an Explorer and a Fixer

Throughout my years of working with others; I have found that one of my biggest strengths is my proclivity for rapid analysis and problem solving. Though deep methodical analysis is not my forte, I can safely say that I am great at finding the most probable cause of an issue, or quickly building various alternative plans to reach an objective.

This has been extremely valuable in numerous situations, including project planning, code debugging, team behavioral analysis when coaching, graphic design prototyping, and quickly bringing together proof of concepts.

### My Stance on AI

At the time of writing this (June 2026), I am of two minds about "AI".

On one side, I am a generally a technophile, and have been testing the limits and value of LLMs tools as they became widely available. I cannot deny their value as a developer, especially for more basic or repetitive tasks (boilerplate code, function documentation, etc.), and even their effectiveness at debugging code that may be unfamiliar, or when I have forgotten the intricacies of some codebase since the time of writing.

On the other hand, I believe that the cost of large scale LLMs is currently too great to justify their widespread adoption. The ecological and ethical concerns alone are enough to give me pause, and I am slowly dialing back my LLM usage as a result.

If I had to draw a line, I would say that, until there is a real system in place for remunerating artists, AI should not be used for direct creative work; we should also be more mindful of how much we rely on LLMs for simple tasks. Yet, I cannot deny the value they bring to specific tasks, like acting as a coding assistant.
