### Context

Anticipating the arrival of a new metro line, a new university campus, and an entirely new town planned for the Plateau de Saclay (south of Paris), our work group was tasked with conceptualizing the area's main train station and accompanying commercial/business center.

Because this station would be among the very first major pieces of infrastructure built in the area, our focus was on envisioning the transit hub not just as a utilitarian stop, but as the foundational "seed" from which the surrounding urban life would grow.

### Spatial Concept & Urban Flow

Traditional elevated train stations often act as physical and visual barriers within a city. Our goal was to dismantle that barrier, creating a natural, seamless flow between the train platforms and the soon-to-be city.

- **Breaking the Barrier:**  
  We designed the platforms to feel like an integrated part of an open public plaza, similar to how modern tramways seamlessly blend in with pedestrian spaces.
- **Balancing Open and Enclosed Spaces:**  
  We intertwined these open, transit-focused areas with more private shopping and work zones. These commercial spaces were structurally sheltered from wind, rain, and noise, yet remained visually connected to the bustling open plaza.

- **Positive and Negative Islands:**  
  To organize this complex program, we utilized a system of "positive" and "negative" architectural islands. Each island was assigned a specific usage (such as parks, restaurants, shops, or outdoor activity spaces) and featured a unique atmosphere. Crucially, these islands served as the vertical connective tissue linking the upper station plaza with the lower shopping and cultural center, naturally guiding foot traffic out of the trains and into the city.

### My Role & Visual Communication

For this project, I was responsible for the final visual polish and presentation. I managed the final assembly, texturing, and rendering for all 2D plans and cross-sections, and produced the high-fidelity 3D perspective renders used to communicate our spatial concepts.  
Additionally, I contributed heavily to the physical construction of the massive scale model our team built for the final presentation.
